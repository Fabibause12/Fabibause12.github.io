Dice games

One device, several people, taking turns. Kniffel keeps the score sheet by itself, in Mäxchen only the player whose turn it is sees the dice, and Anna’s dice game keeps track of the pot. Nothing is saved – reloading the page ends every game.

Kniffel

One to six players, 13 rounds, three rolls per round.

Round 1 of 13

–

3 Würfe übrig

Erst würfeln.

Tapping an empty box enters the points shown and ends the turn. A zero is allowed too.

Rules as they apply here
  • Five dice, three rolls per turn. Between rolls you may keep as many dice as you like.
  • Upper section: Ones to Sixes count the pips of the matching dice. From 63 points on there is a bonus of 35 points.
  • Lower section: Three of a kind and Four of a kind count the sum of all five dice, Full House 25, Small straight 30, Large straight 40, Kniffel 50, Chance the sum of all dice.
  • Here a Kniffel does not count as a Full House or a straight.
  • Every further Kniffel earns 50 bonus points if the Kniffel box already holds 50. You still have to fill in a box somewhere.
  • Every box is filled exactly once. If nothing fits, a box is crossed out with 0.

Mäxchen

Two to six players, two dice, lots of lying. The device gets passed around.

    Rules as they apply here
    • Order of the rolls, from low to high: 31, 32, 41, 42, 43, 51, 52, 53, 54, 61, 62, 63, 64, 65, then the doubles 11 to 66 and at the very top the Mäxchen 21.
    • Whoever rolls sees the result alone and then announces a value. It has to be higher than the announcement before – the truth is optional.
    • The next player believes it and rolls on, or calls the bluff.
    • When the bluff is called: if the real roll is at least as high as the announcement, the caller loses a life. Otherwise the announcer loses one.
    • After an announced Mäxchen the bluff has to be called. If it was true, the caller loses two lives.
    • At zero lives you’re out. The loser starts the new round, and the announcements start again from zero.

    Anna’s dice game

    Two to six players, two dice, one shared pot. Bank in time – or lose it all on a seven.

    Round 1 of 15

    –

    In the bank 0

    What was rolled? Tap the sum.

      Anyone who hasn’t banked yet can tap “Bank” at any time – not only the player whose turn it is.

      Rules as they apply here
      • Every round starts with an empty bank. Players take turns rolling two dice, and every roll goes into the one shared bank.
      • The first three rolls of a round are safe: a seven is worth 70 points, everything else – doubles included – counts its pips.
      • From the fourth roll on, doubles double the bank and a seven ends the round. Anyone who hasn’t banked by then gets nothing.
      • “Bank” adds the current bank to your own score. After that you sit out until the end of the round, and the bank keeps growing for the others.
      • The round is also over once everyone has banked. The next round is started by the next player in line.
      • After the last round, whoever has the most points wins.
      • With your own dice you roll at the table and tap the sum. From the fourth roll on, doubles go on the “Doubles” button – that’s why 2 and 12 are greyed out then. The arrows at the top step back and forward, for example after a wrong entry.
